## Black History Month 

By Danika Pierce (10)

Black History Month is an annually observed commemorative month during February, but it is often misinterpreted. Many may recall the lessons in history class of the sufferings of enslaved people on sugar plantations. Under this notion, one may be drawn to believe that the month is an apology. However, the formal and true purpose of Black History Month is to honor black people and their impact. 
 Our school has followed this intention. The school  prepared activities for Black History Month, such  as replicating artworks by  black artists for the third through seventh graders and exploring soul music for the eighth through tenth graders. The historical analysis occurs under the guidance of Dr.Theis. Respectfully replicating the artworks allows the students to connect with the art to a greater extent than simple observation. According to Ms. Hinton, the heritage months are an opportunity  for learning about others. She shared that the activities were chosen with a certain perspective to  respect the innovations by African-Americans in  many ways, such as art, music, and so on. 
In celebration of this heritage month, recognition shall be given to Harlem writer, Langston Hughes. The Harlem writers were black writers that gained prominence during the Harlem Renaissance, which was an African-American cultural and intellectual movement centered in Harlem, Manhattan. This  movement occurred during the 1920s and 1930s. Langston Hughes is widely  considered a leader of this movement. Receiver of a  Bachelors of Arts degree  from Lincoln University, he had numerous accomplishments, including being an innovator of jazz poetry, social activist, novelist, poet, playwright, and columnist. Jazz poetry has a sense of improvisation and is intended to be performed. It later evolved into modern-day poetry slams, which is live poetry, and hip-hop music. Hughes had often been criticized for revealing the “ugly” side of black lives. However, his writing has ultimately been recognized as black pride.
