## History of Valentine's Day

By Rebecca Guo (7Mn)

Every February 14, millions celebrate Valentine’s Day with cards, flowers, and declarations of love, but the holiday’s roots stretch far deeper than heart-shaped chocolates.Valentine’s Day actually developed over centuries from a mix of Roman ritual, Christian tradition, and medieval romance. The exact origin of Valentine’s Day is unknown, but we do know that the holiday was named after St. Valentines. A popular legend claims he was secretly performing marriages for soldiers, which was banned by the Roman emperor at that time. He was executed around 269 CE, and then in 496 CE Pope Gelasius I declared February 14th Valentine’s Day, possibly to honor St. Valentines and christianize early Roman holidays. The connection to romantic love was in the 1300s, where poet Geoffrey Chaucer linked St. Valentine’s Day with love in his poetry. At this time, Europe started associating mid-February with the mating season of birds, reinforcing the romantic theme. By the 15th century, nobles exchanged written “valentines” (love notes) among each other. In the 18th and 19th century, Valentine's day became what we know today. Handwritten cards became popular, and the rise of mass printing in the 1800s led to commercially produced cards. Today Valentine’s Day is now widely celebrated in many countries as a day focused on romantic relationships, friendship, and gift-giving.
