## Worker Unions!

By Danika Pierce (10)

 International Workers day, which is referred to as Labor Day in some countries and often as May Day, is May 1st. May Day is regarded as a national holiday in numerous countries. In the United States, Labor Day, September 7th, is an international holiday instead. The holiday is in celebration of the labour movement and generally the working class. May Day can also serve as an example of how greatly labor has changed. Celebrations for the holiday include the traditional European spring festival of May Day. The festival had existed before May Day and was intended as a way for agriculturalists to celebrate the flowering in hopes for a good harvest. In the United States, May Day celebrates the Haymarket Affair. On May 4, 1886, a peaceful rally at Haymarket Square advocated for the eight-hour work day. Someone, who remains unknown to this day, threw a dynamite bomb at the police. Eight anarchists ended up being charged with the bombing and convicted of conspiracy, though there had been no actual evidence that any of these individuals threw the bomb. Following the Haymarket Affair, labor unions in the United States, such as the Knights of Labor, lost popularity and employers generally gained better control over the working class. The modern labor force is far different than that of 1886, which is due to social and technological changes. Over the course of American history, labor laws have been implemented. For instance, the Fair Labor Standards Act, which was last revised in 2024, created standards for wages and overtime pay. Under these notions, labor conditions have greatly improved. However, the labor unions of the United States have had a decline in membership. This ultimately harms the ability of the working class to collectively advocate for themselves. Additionally, technological advancements, such as the internet, have allowed work to expand out of work hours. It is extremely easy for a person to be called, messaged, emailed, and so on out of office. Before carrying a cellphone everywhere had become common practice, the line between work and leisure had been clearer. The question is not whether or not the conditions of labor have worsened. Instead, it is simply that labor has changed itself.
